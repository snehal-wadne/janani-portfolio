# My Life, My Family, and My Dreams

*The full story, in Janani's own words. Use this for the long-form "Read her full story" page.*

## 1. My Life, My Family, and My Dreams

My name is Janani Pradhaban. I am a classical Bharatanatyam dancer and teacher, a wife, a mother, and a liver transplant recipient. My life has been a journey through dreams, unexpected challenges, immense suffering, renewed hope, and profound gratitude.

I was born into a family associated with India's Defence Production establishment. My father, Mr. G. Pradhaban, is a retired Indian Ordnance Factories Service (IOFS) officer, and my mother is Mrs. Malini Pradhaban. My parents played an important role in shaping my life, encouraging me to explore education, develop my talents, and appreciate the different arts.

I have always been a treasured child to my father. The love and encouragement I received from my family became an important foundation throughout my life.

From the age of four, I began learning Bharatanatyam. What started as a childhood interest soon became my greatest passion. Dance was not simply an extracurricular activity; it became an essential part of my identity, a source of happiness, and a dream I wanted to pursue throughout my life.

## 2. Bharatanatyam: The Dream That Shaped Me

My early years in dance were filled with excitement and achievement. I began performing on prestigious stages, at temple festivals, during cultural celebrations, and on special occasions. Each performance brought new experiences, confidence, and joy.

My love for the performing arts continued to grow. Alongside Bharatanatyam, I explored Western dance, Kathak, and Odissi. I wanted to learn, perform, and understand different styles of movement and expression.

I successfully completed my Salangai Pujai and Arangetram, important milestones in my Bharatanatyam journey. Through dedication and continuous practice, I developed my skills and began gaining recognition and appreciation for my performances.

At one stage, I was performing almost twice a week. I felt as though I was living the life I had always dreamed of. The stage was a place where I could express myself freely, and every performance gave me another reason to work harder.

I even dreamed of releasing my own dance album. My family and I began working towards making that dream a reality, investing time, effort, and dedication in the process.

I believed that my future would be filled with dance, teaching, performances, and new artistic achievements.

But life had a different path in store for me.

## 3. The Unexpected Diagnosis

From the age of thirteen, I began experiencing numerous health problems. My body was affected by serious medical complications, and my ability to move and function normally gradually declined.

The changes were difficult to understand and even more difficult to accept. I was young, full of dreams, and deeply committed to dance. Yet I began losing the ability to participate in the activities that had once defined my everyday life.

Eventually, I received a diagnosis that changed everything: liver cirrhosis.

I struggled to believe what I was hearing. I had never had jaundice, and I had been practising classical dance regularly. I initially thought there might have been a mistake in my test results.

But the diagnosis was real.

My dreams seemed to collapse in front of me. The thought of losing dance, the future I had imagined, and perhaps even the life I wanted to live was overwhelming. My family was devastated, and we began facing a reality none of us had expected.

Gradually, my health challenges became a constant part of everyday life. There were new difficulties, new concerns, and continuing uncertainty.

Despite everything, I tried not to allow my illness to take away my desire to live. I wanted to preserve whatever happiness I could find and hold on to the hope that my circumstances might one day improve.

## 4. Marriage, Love, and Family Support

At the age of twenty-four, I married Suresh Marisamy.

Marriage brought a new chapter into my life. Although my health challenges continued, my husband stood by me and supported me through difficult times. His encouragement and the love of my family helped me manage the uncertainty surrounding my future.

However, my illness gradually progressed. My physical condition became increasingly difficult to manage, and the need for a liver transplant became more urgent.

The doctors explained the risks associated with the surgery, its possible complications, and the importance of transplantation for my survival. The prospect of such a major operation was frightening for me and my family.

Yet the alternative was even more frightening.

Without a transplant, my life was in serious danger.

## 5. Five Years of Waiting for a Liver

For five long years, my family and I waited for a suitable donor liver.

Those five years were among the most difficult of my life. Every day brought uncertainty. We lived with the physical burden of the disease and the emotional strain of not knowing when an organ might become available.

We prayed, visited places of worship, and held on to hope. Our family continued to support me, even as everyone carried their own fears and worries.

My health deteriorated further. Before the transplant, I had lost much of my hair, my nails had turned dark, and my skin had undergone significant changes. Even ordinary daily activities became extremely difficult.

There were times when I could barely move. I felt that I was losing my independence and the ability to participate in the life I had once enjoyed.

The experience was physically exhausting and emotionally painful.

Throughout this period, the support of my medical team and the encouragement of my family helped me continue through the difficult waiting period and prepare for the possibility of transplantation.

I was frightened, but I continued to hope.

For me, the wait was not simply about receiving an organ. It was about the possibility of recovering my health, returning to my passion, spending more years with my family, and discovering whether the dreams I had lost could somehow return.

## 6. The Midnight Call That Changed My Life

Then, one night, everything changed.

At approximately 12:30 a.m., we received a call from the hospital. A family whose loved one had been declared brain-dead had agreed to donate organs. We were asked to reach the hospital within an hour.

The news was overwhelming.

After five years of waiting, a possibility of survival had finally arrived. At that moment, I felt immense gratitude towards the donor and the family who had made this decision.

We immediately left for the hospital. I was admitted at approximately 2:00 a.m., and the night continued with medical examinations, tests, and preparations for surgery.

My family and relatives were deeply anxious. They had spent years watching me struggle and now faced the uncertainty of a major operation. Their prayers and tears reflected the love and fear they carried in their hearts.

I remember feeling especially grateful for the people surrounding me. Even though my health was failing, I was fortunate to have a family that continued to stand beside me.

My father remained strong and hopeful. His belief that I would return home gave me strength during an uncertain time.

For the first time in a long while, the possibility of a new beginning felt real.

## 7. 9th February 2016: My Second Birthday

On the morning of 9 February 2016, at approximately 9:00 a.m., I was taken to the operating theatre for my liver transplant.

As I entered, I closed my eyes and prayed to the Almighty:

Please give me a new life. My family is waiting for me outside.

The operation lasted approximately twelve hours.

During this time, my family and loved ones waited anxiously. More than twenty people, known and unknown to us, came forward to donate blood and plasma in support of my treatment. The generosity of these individuals became another important part of my journey.

Finally, the surgery was successful.

That evening, at approximately 10:00 p.m., I was transferred to the intensive care unit.

After five years of waiting, I had received a liver transplant.

The experience was made possible by the generosity of the deceased donor's family and the coordinated efforts of the medical team: the doctors, nurses, and other healthcare professionals who cared for me.

I will always remember that day as the beginning of my second life.

## 8. Waking Up to a New World

When I opened my eyes in the intensive care unit, I found myself surrounded by glass walls, medical equipment, wires, and large machines. Nurses were caring for me, monitoring my condition and helping me through the first stage of recovery.

The environment was unfamiliar, and my body was weak after the surgery.

But when I saw my family, I felt a happiness that is difficult to describe.

The people I loved were there. I had survived the operation, and I had another opportunity to live.

After five uncertain days, I was transferred to the ward. My hospital stay continued to involve medicines, injections, medical tests, consultations, and the daily challenges of recovery.

My illness had taken a toll on my entire family. My mother had lost a significant amount of weight during the stressful period, and it was painful for my father to watch me struggle through treatment.

Their suffering reminded me that serious illness affects not only the patient but also everyone who loves and cares for that person.

Eventually, the day came when I was discharged from the hospital.

Returning home felt like entering a new world.

For me, it was a rebirth.

## 9. Recovery and the Return of My Dreams

After returning home, I had to take precautions for several months to reduce the risk of infection. Visitors were restricted during the first six months, and I followed the medical advice provided for my recovery.

Slowly, my body began to change for the better.

My hair started growing back. My nails became healthier, and my skin gradually recovered from the changes I had experienced during my illness. My strength and overall well-being improved.

Every small sign of recovery felt meaningful.

The opportunity to perform simple daily activities without the same level of difficulty was a blessing. Things that many people take for granted had become precious to me.

And then came a moment I had once feared might never happen.

I began dancing again.

Bharatanatyam had been part of my life since I was four years old. My return to dance represented more than the recovery of a physical ability. It represented the return of hope, identity, and a part of myself I thought I might have lost forever.

I could once again look towards the future with a sense of possibility.

I remain deeply grateful to the donor and the donor's family. Although I may never meet them, I feel that their generosity continues to shape my life every day. I honour their decision by living with gratitude and purpose.

## 10. The Gift of Motherhood

After my transplant, I began to think about the future in a way I had not been able to during the most difficult years of my illness.

One of my deepest hopes was to become a mother.

Motherhood represented a new chapter, a new responsibility, and a new dream. It meant the possibility of nurturing a child, sharing love, and building a future with my family.

On 28 September 2017, I gave birth to a healthy son.

This was one of the most precious moments of my life.

After years of illness, uncertainty, and waiting for a donor, I had the opportunity to experience motherhood. The arrival of my child gave a new meaning to the life I had been given.

Dr. Mohamed Rela carried my son in his arms and blessed him with a gold chain. A miracle in the very next year: February 2016 was my transplantation, and in 2017 my son was born.

Today, my family, my child, and my passion for Bharatanatyam are among the greatest blessings in my life.

I do not take any of these experiences for granted.

Every ordinary day is precious because I know how uncertain life can be.

## 11. My Gratitude to the Donor and the Donor's Family

There is one family to whom I will always owe a debt of gratitude.

They made the decision to donate the organs of a loved one during a time of profound grief. They could not have known all the details of my life, my dreams, or the future I hoped to build.

Yet their decision gave me an opportunity that I might otherwise never have received.

Because of their generosity, I could return to my family, dance again, and become a mother.

I may never know their names or meet them personally. Nevertheless, their decision remains a permanent part of my life story.

I think of the donor with gratitude every day.

I also recognise the generosity of the individuals who donated blood and plasma during my surgery and the dedication of the doctors, nurses, and healthcare professionals who helped me recover.

My second chance at life was made possible through compassion, medical expertise, and the support of many people.

For this, I will always be thankful.

## 12. Understanding the True Meaning of Organ Donation

My experience has taught me that organ donation is one of the most meaningful ways in which people can help others.

Organ donation may offer patients with organ failure a chance of survival or a better quality of life, depending on their medical circumstances and the transplant outcome.

A donor may be able to help multiple recipients, subject to medical suitability and the organs and tissues available for donation.

Behind every patient waiting for an organ is a human story. There are parents hoping to watch their children grow, young people wishing to fulfil their dreams, spouses hoping to share more years together, and families praying for another chance.

I understand that people may feel uncertain about organ donation. They may have questions about the process, religious or cultural beliefs, or concerns about what happens after death.

These concerns should be addressed through respectful conversations and reliable information from qualified healthcare professionals and authorised organisations.

I encourage everyone to learn about organ donation, discuss their wishes with family members, and consider registering as an organ donor if they decide that it is right for them.

A conversation today may help a family understand a loved one's wishes in the future.

## 13. Why I Want to Volunteer with MOHAN Foundation

I wish to volunteer with MOHAN Foundation because organ donation is not just a cause I believe in. It is a cause that has touched every part of my life.

I know what it means to wait for a donor organ. I know how difficult it can be for a family to live with uncertainty. I know how frightening the prospect of transplantation can feel, and I know the hope that a successful transplant can bring.

My experience gives me a personal perspective that I would like to share with others.

Through volunteering, I hope to contribute to organ donation awareness, help people understand the importance of informed decisions, and encourage families to discuss their wishes openly.

I would also like to share my story with patients and families who may be facing the challenges of transplantation. If my experience can provide encouragement to even one person, I would consider that meaningful.

I see volunteering as a way to express gratitude to my donor, honour the donor's family, and give something back to society.

I would be proud to support MOHAN Foundation's awareness efforts, learn from its team, and contribute wherever my experience and abilities can be useful.

## 14. My Message to the World

My life has taught me that hope can survive even in the most difficult circumstances.

I went from a young dancer with dreams of a future on stage to a patient facing a life-threatening illness. I spent five years waiting for a suitable donor liver. On 9 February 2016, I received the transplant that gave me another chance at life.

I returned to Bharatanatyam. I became a mother on 28 September 2017. I continue to enjoy the blessings of family, health, and the opportunity to pursue my passions.

I know that my story would have been very different without organ donation.

That is why I ask people to learn about this life-giving act, discuss their wishes with their families, and help spread awareness.

Organ donation is a deeply personal decision, and everyone deserves access to accurate information to make that decision. But whatever decision people make, I hope they will approach the subject with openness, compassion, and understanding.

Life is precious. Every person has dreams, relationships, and a future worth cherishing.

For those waiting for an organ, the generosity of a donor and the donor's family may offer the possibility of another chapter.

I received a gift that changed my life. I want to honour that gift by helping others understand the power of organ donation.

My journey is a story of survival, but it is also a story of human kindness.

To my donor, to the donor's family, to my own family, and to everyone who supported my treatment and recovery, I offer my heartfelt gratitude.

May my story encourage more people to learn, talk, and consider how they can help others.

A gift of life can become a lifetime of hope.

Thank you.

*— Janani Pradhaban*
