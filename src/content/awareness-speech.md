# A Gift of Life: My Journey from Waiting to Living

*An organ donation awareness speech by Janani Pradhaban. Use for the "Her Voice / Speech" section (collapsible or separate page).*

Respected dignitaries, doctors, healthcare professionals, members of MOHAN Foundation, and my dear friends,

Good morning to everyone.

My name is Janani Pradhaban. I am a classical Bharatanatyam dancer and teacher, a wife, a mother, and a proud liver transplant recipient.

Today, I stand before you not merely to speak about organ donation, but to share a story that has shaped every part of my life.

Ten years ago, on 9 February 2016, I received a second chance at life through a liver transplant. Today, I can smile, spend time with my loved ones, teach and practise Bharatanatyam, and look forward to the future.

I am here because someone made a decision that changed my life forever.

A family, in the middle of its deepest grief, agreed to donate the organs of a loved one. Their decision gave me the opportunity to live again.

I may never meet that family. I may never be able to thank them personally. But every day, I carry immense gratitude for their generosity.

My life is a reminder that one family's decision to donate can become another family's reason to hope.

Let me take you back to the beginning of my journey.

I was born into a family associated with India's Defence Production establishment. My father, Mr. G. Pradhaban, is a retired Indian Ordnance Factories Service officer, and my mother is Mrs. Malini Pradhaban. I grew up surrounded by love, encouragement, and opportunities to explore education and the arts.

From the age of four, Bharatanatyam became my greatest passion. I began learning dance, performing on prestigious stages, and participating in temple festivals, cultural celebrations, and special occasions. I also explored Western dance, Kathak, and Odissi.

I successfully completed my Salangai Pujai and Arangetram and worked towards building my future in dance. I performed frequently and even dreamed of releasing my own dance album.

But life took an unexpected turn.

From the age of thirteen, I began experiencing serious health problems. Eventually, I was diagnosed with liver cirrhosis. I struggled to understand how this could happen when dance had been such an important part of my life.

My health deteriorated, and I had to give up dancing and many activities that once brought me happiness. My dreams became uncertain, and my family experienced immense emotional pain.

I married my husband, Suresh Marisamy, at the age of twenty-four. His support became an important source of strength as I continued to face my health challenges.

For five long years, we waited for a suitable liver donor. Each day brought uncertainty. My body became weaker, and even ordinary daily activities became difficult. We prayed, hoped, and waited, knowing that without a transplant my life was in serious danger.

During this period, the support of my medical team and my family helped me continue through the difficult waiting period.

Then came the night that changed everything.

At approximately 12:30 a.m., we received a call from the hospital. A deceased donor's family had agreed to donate organs, and we needed to reach the hospital within an hour.

In that moment, I felt that a new possibility had opened before me.

I reached the hospital and underwent the necessary tests. On the morning of 9 February 2016, I was taken into the operating theatre.

Before the surgery, I closed my eyes and prayed to the Almighty to give me a new life and allow me to return to my family.

The operation lasted approximately twelve hours. My family waited anxiously, while friends and relatives came forward to donate blood and plasma to support my treatment.

Finally, the surgery was successful.

When I regained consciousness in the intensive care unit, I saw medical equipment and wires around me, with nurses caring for me. Later, when I saw my family, my heart filled with happiness and relief.

Recovery was not easy. There were medicines, injections, tests, medical reviews, and many days of patience. My family had endured years of worry, and seeing me recover brought hope after a long period of uncertainty.

After I returned home, I had to take precautions to protect myself from infection. Gradually, my body began to recover. My strength returned, my hair grew back, and my overall health improved.

And then, something I had once feared I might never experience happened.

I began dancing again.

Bharatanatyam, which had been part of my life since childhood, returned as a symbol of hope and recovery.

Another extraordinary blessing followed. On 28 September 2017, I gave birth to a healthy son. Dr. Mohamed Rela carried my son in his arms and blessed him with a gold chain. Becoming a mother gave me a new understanding of the preciousness of life.

Today, when I spend time with my child and family, teach dance, or simply enjoy an ordinary day, I remember how uncertain my future once was.

That is why organ donation means so much to me.

Organ donation is not simply a medical process. It is an opportunity to give another person a future. It can help a child continue growing with a parent, enable a family to remain together, and give a patient the possibility of returning to the life they love.

One donor may be able to save multiple lives through organ donation, depending on medical suitability and the organs donated.

Yet many people remain uncertain about organ donation. Some are afraid. Others have questions about medical procedures, family consent, cultural practices, or religious beliefs.

These questions deserve respectful answers and accurate information. We should talk openly with our families, learn from qualified healthcare professionals, and make informed decisions about our wishes.

As a transplant recipient, I want people to understand that behind every patient waiting for an organ is a human being with dreams, responsibilities, relationships, and hopes for tomorrow.

I also want to acknowledge the donor families. Their generosity often comes at one of the most painful moments of their lives. Their decision deserves profound respect and gratitude.

I can never repay the gift I received. But I can honour it by living meaningfully and helping others understand the importance of organ donation.

That is one of the reasons I wish to volunteer with MOHAN Foundation. I want to share my experience, support awareness initiatives, and encourage more people to learn about organ donation.

My dear friends, I would like to leave you with three simple requests.

First, learn about organ donation from reliable sources.

Second, talk to your family about your wishes. Open conversations can help family members understand and respect one another's decisions.

Third, consider registering as an organ donor if that is your informed choice, and understand the applicable procedures.

We may not all have the opportunity to change the world in a dramatic way. But we may have the opportunity to help change one person's future.

For five years, I waited for a donor. Today, I am alive because a family said yes.

Their decision gave me back my life, my dance, and the opportunity to become a mother.

I carry that gratitude with me every day.

Life is precious. Compassion is powerful. And organ donation can turn the pain of one family's loss into hope for another family.

Let us learn. Let us talk. Let us spread awareness. Let us help save lives.

Organ donation gave me a second chance at life. Together, we can help give others that same hope.

Thank you so much.

Jai Hind.
